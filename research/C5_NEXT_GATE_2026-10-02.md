## Proposed bounded operational test

**Hypothesis:** 64 Lean verifier workers can run alongside the loaded C5 actor,
reference policy, and rollout backend on a four-H100 full node without memory
failure, changed verifier semantics, or a throughput collapse that removes the
potential runtime margin.

Use the pinned C5 model, archive, data, verifier, reward rule, and limits. Run
only a short, explicitly labeled operational prefix with 64 workers under
`def-zhijing`. Cap the diagnostic training process at 6,000 seconds within
the separately requested 24-hour workspace allocation. Record the exact runner and
source hashes, GPU memory, node available memory, verifier process count,
per-step generation/verification/update timings, proof counts, verifier
failures, and exit cause. Stop at a predeclared completed-step boundary; do not
use its partial trajectory for C5 science or resume it as the full run. Compare
failure categories and timing distribution to the historical 32-worker prefix,
while acknowledging that generated proofs and node conditions may differ.

The operational pass gate is: the actor and verifier coexist without OOM,
worker death, or a new verifier failure pattern; every completed step has
internally consistent proposal and reward-rejection accounting; and observed
verification timing supports a conservative full-run margin. A short prefix
alone cannot establish late-tail feasibility. If it passes, assess whether a
fixed late-proof panel can be replayed with the actor resident and whether the
checkpoint recovery gates in `CHECKPOINT_RECOVERY_AUDIT.md` are satisfied
before authorizing another full C5 run. If it fails, diagnose the limiting
resource or tail rather than repeating the full run.

## October 4 outcome and decision

Workspace `1018446` ran on `trig0012` from October 2 22:15 to October 3 22:15
EDT, then reached its 24-hour `sleep infinity` limit. The diagnostic completed
20 C5 steps in 2,863.345 seconds and stopped at its registered boundary. The
stop sent SIGTERM after step 20; the child ended with return code `-6` during
teardown. The completed-step log and stop status precede that exit. No
checkpoint or proof-level C5 result
was expected or produced.

The frozen gate output is
`results/c5_actorloaded64_feasibility_1018446.json`. All 20 groups account for
512 proposals, blocked and reward-rejected counts match, and no fatal runtime
pattern appears before the stop. Minimum sampled node `MemAvailable` was
559.648 GiB, peak sampled GPU memory was 62,337 MiB, and the peak sampled
REPL-related process count was 128. Actor-loaded memory therefore passes this
bounded screen.

The throughput gate fails. First-20 verification sums are 1,562.274 seconds
in historical 32-worker C5 and 1,646.096 seconds in this 64-worker prefix:
0.949x old/new. The registered rough timing calculation yields 26.097 logged
hours for 604 steps, above its 22-hour screen. This is only a planning
calculation: the two runs used different nodes and generated trajectories.

A post-hoc step decomposition locates the discrepancy. Excluding steps 3 and
6, the 18 other batches total 1,405.263 seconds at 32 workers and 975.437
seconds at 64 workers, a 1.441x ratio. The new steps 3 and 6 instead took
331.573 and 339.086 seconds, versus 82.713 and 74.298 seconds historically.
Their duration is consistent with a 300-second verifier timeout tail; the
partial diagnostic saved no per-proof verdict log, so that cause remains
unverified. The similar aggregate generated-proof error counts do not isolate
the tail mechanism. The fixed-proof model-free benchmark already showed a
1.385x worker-speedup with identical verdicts; this actor-loaded result adds
memory feasibility and exposes tail risk under live generation.

**Decision:** Do not submit another full C5 restart from this evidence. The
next discriminating operational test would preserve the actor in memory while
replaying identical fixed proofs at 32 and 64 workers, including late or
timeout-prone panels, with per-proof failure classes and timings saved. This
tests whether the two live tails reflect proof composition or a 64-worker
runtime issue. The separate exact-checkpoint-recovery gates still apply before
an eligible long C5 run. No replacement job is queued.

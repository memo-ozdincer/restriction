# Hard sorting bottleneck pilot

Hypothesis registered before calibration/training: a fixed nearest-neighbor sorting-network interconnect with a tight optimal comparator budget can make ordinary terminal-reward policy learning discovery-limited even over a long budget, while uniformly random legal prefix resets may provide transferable experience without reference networks. Because the comparator graph is a path, the worst-case reverse ordering requires n(n-1)/2 adjacent swaps and an ordinary adjacent sorting network achieves that bound. Exact reachable state enumeration and a random-action finite-horizon hitting probability will independently check the optimum and quantify the discovery barrier. We test six-, seven-, and eight-wire path instances in calibration and choose the larger practical state graph for learning based on state count and ordinary random reward accessibility, not learned outcomes. The trained actor only sees the remaining binary-row set; BFS distance is never input or reward.

Explicitly track true terminal hits and first discovery, root checkpoint and fresh final success, independently verify successful sequences. If initial results suggest a meaningful advantage, compare longer matched trajectories with a prespecified higher update budget instead of merely replicating. This is a small stochastic policy fixed-instance experiment, not an LLM or generalization result.

Calibration established 8-wire adjacent-only root p=1.05674e-10 (approximately one random success in 9.46 billion trajectories), versus 6-wire p=9.60e-6 and 7-wire p=5.02e-8. Eight-wire reachable state graph has 40,320 states and fits in memory. Select that target, radius 12 (mean shortest distance 23.23, initial p=4.42e-5) and radius 18 (21.61, p=6.44e-4), and radius 24 (20.14, p=.003915) as a spectrum of still-hard legal perturbations with usable initial reward. Radius 6 is too sparse (6.22e-7) at the intended pilot budget, so use it only if longer experiments become justified. Fixed 2,000 updates x 64 episodes x 28 action slots, seed 42..49, ordinary, three walk radii continued and 30%-switch. The radius selection was made on exact random-policy accessibility only, before training. Exact final root success from the learned policy is computed by absorbing DP over 40,320 native states, alongside 512 fresh independently verified samples and time-to-first reward. Prespecified criterion useful for follow-up: if variation produces >=10% original root success in any seed while ordinary is discovery-starved, widen the budget without changing training settings to find whether ordinary catches up.

Ordinary policies encountered *zero training rewards* across 1,024,000 episodes & 28,672,000 policy action slots, and their final parameters are bit-identical to initialization. Every ordinary checkpoint evaluation was also zero. All assisted radius18 seeds discovered a first terminal reward by update 86 (median 9.5). Before restoring the original start at update 600, radius18 had already learned an average 55.03% ordinary-start success in sampled evaluations, before any designated ordinary-root training. (A random walk can leave the state unchanged, so an occasional reset may coincide with root, but none is selected for being a useful route.) Root specialization subsequently raises success to about 99.4%. Radius18 switched has 2,920,487 reset transitions in addition to 27,398,728 active policy actions versus 28,672,000 ordinary active actions, about 5.74% extra total active transitions despite matched padded action slots. Radius18 switched final fresh successes contain 949 distinct optimal comparator sequences.

Radius12 switched twice removed resets before any positive reward was observed: seeds 42/46 then never got any, and remained bit-identical to their initial policies by the same zero-gradient argument. The matching continued-radius12 policies did discover eventual rewards and obtain 60.9%/89.6% exact ordinary performance. This is direct evidence that a useful exposure phase must survive the first-discovery bottleneck, not simply consume a fixed fraction of updates. The stronger radius18/24 conditions solve in all seeds.

## Analytic first-reward waiting time (ordinary controls)

Because the baseline is terminal-only REINFORCE without an entropy term, all-zero terminal batches supply exactly zero gradient and Adam's initial moments stay zero. The ordinary policy cannot change before its first successful sampled trajectory. Therefore for a frozen initial policy with exact goal-hitting probability p, the probability of any first success in N independent trajectories is 1-(1-p)^N. No assumption about post-success learning is needed. The exact dynamic-programming probabilities span 5.815e-11 to 2.068e-10 across the eight actual initial policies; mean waiting time 4.84–17.20 billion complete trajectories (75.6–268.7 million batches of 64). At 60,000 updates, first-hit probability is only 0.0223%–0.0794%; at one million updates it is 0.371%–1.315%. These are model-based exact-policy probabilities under independent stochastic sampling, not executed 60k/1m update experiments. They are more informative than further brute-force null runs: analytically, even a 500-fold ordinary budget has very little chance to produce the first nonzero gradient. This conclusion depends on the tested actor/optimizer having zero updates before reward, and does not apply to arbitrary larger policies, pretraining or alternative estimators.

The finite graph has exactly 48,608,795,688,960 optimal root sequences among 7^28=459,986,536,544,739,960,976,801 28-action sequences. An independent integer shortest-path count confirms the uniform random probability 1.0567438789424684e-10. The optimum comes from exact BFS plus reverse-order input lower bound. All 21,331 final fresh wins across all 56 runs replayed through an independent scalar checker over all 256 binary input combinations, and all saved final probabilities reproduce under exact finite-horizon DP. No auxiliary objective, process signal, action mask, human comparator ordering, reference solution, or distance-based reset selection entered training. Radius selection was informed by theoretical initial random success accessibility (not trained results). Random legal walks supply an easier starting-state distribution and are not equal-distance controls. Primitive action slots are matched, actual transitions differ. One fixed task and one small actor, no generalization or LLM claim.

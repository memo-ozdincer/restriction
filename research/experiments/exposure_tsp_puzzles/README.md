# CPU pilot: related TSP subproblems and native Flip resets

## Registered hypotheses (before implementation tests)

1. **TSP.** Uniform subsets of one fixed, hard full geometric instance may expose productive improvement trajectories that the full-instance policy rarely encounters. A switch back to full-instance improvement may eventually outperform uninterrupted full-instance RL at equal sampled action slots. The final full-instance objective and 2-opt dynamics are restored exactly. Subsets *change the training task* (they are derived auxiliary TSP instances), not the terminal objective on the original instance. We will measure whether auxiliary groups have nonzero advantages and whether gains persist after withdrawal. The full instance is published NICO `tsp_unif50_test_seed1234.pkl` row 0, selected by index before inspecting trajectories. Published Concorde reference is used only as evaluation metric. We use a lightweight CPU linear-policy control, not a reproduction of NICO weights or architecture. Its 2-opt moves and group relative improvement reward follow the relevant mechanics of NICO. The fixed initial distribution is random tours improved to a strict greedy 2-opt local minimum; all modes use the same deterministic warmup per sampled problem. Uniform auxiliary cardinalities 20..40 inclusive, cities sampled without replacement, no quality filtering or teacher. Exactly one shared start per rollout group. Full-only, subsets-only, and subsets-to-full (switch at 30%) modes; paired seeds, identical initialization and optimizer, same action horizon, group size and update count. Additional full-instance root evaluation for all arms throughout, with fixed common RNG schedule. Warmup move count and candidate-score evaluations are recorded; equal padded rollout slots does not imply equal per-step operations across sizes. Full evaluation uses native terminal tour length, returns best-so-far as NICO does. We do not claim NICO-level performance or held-out generalization from this single instance. If every arm gets essentially no signal, or auxiliary signal does not improve full-task performance, that is discriminating negative evidence.

2. **Flip (cross-shaped Lights Out).** The PUZZLES benchmark includes Flip and reports unsolved/random-level performance on several puzzles; our isolated fixed cross-pattern subenvironment is mechanically related, not a reproduction of its DreamerV3 baseline. Random starts generated by xor with independently sampled legal button toggles near the all-lit goal grant genuine intermediate state exposure without showing a solution path. All policies have the same 3x3 board, deterministic transitions, horizon and binary terminal reward. Fixed root generated by the first state in integer order with maximum exact minimum button count on the solvable component, determined by an offline finite graph audit (this choice is for difficulty, not training). To keep exhaustive certification manageable, the experiment uses a 3x3 cross board initially, whose state space is at most 512. Full root, uniform-random-state, uniform-to-root, near-goal reset, near-to-root arms; near walks 1..4 steps, reject solved reset. Every trial sees one shared start per group and independently sampled actions. Compare switch at 30%; full root evaluation unchanged throughout. Hypothesis: near-state reward access may lead to retained skill, while uniform starts may be too difficult and even high auxiliary reward may not transfer. No distances, oracle actions, or partial rewards available to learner. Exact toy verification and independent replay. The wider 5x5 version is deliberately prospective, not part of this registered run.

Source context: NICO-TSP https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06940v1, code https://github.com/TheLeprechaun25/nico-tsp. PUZZLES https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00401 and https://github.com/ETH-DISCO/rlp , whose `experiment_commands.txt` uses `flip --arg 3x3c`. Existing reset curricula and near-goal sampling have substantial overlap. This is a diagnosis of reward access and retained ability, not an algorithmic novelty claim.

No cluster job has been submitted; parent coordinates one full-node batch under `def-zhijing` following `cluster/TRILLIUM_CPU_QUICKSTART.md`. Local smoke tests are restricted to correctness and mechanics.

Compute refinement before main runs: TSP scores all 1,175 padded candidate slots on every policy step for both full and subset modes, masks nonexistent moves and computes score gradients over equal-sized arrays. Actual legal candidate counts plus warmup comparisons (including final stopping comparison) are separately logged. Geometry preprocessing and warmup work remain smaller for subset states; this is equal policy computation/action slots, not equal exact wall time. For Flip all nine actions scored regardless of early solution; active actions and reset action costs logged separately.

Local feasibility-only audit after implementation: 64 seed-20261127 full-instance greedy warmups all above published reference; gap min 0.75%, mean 8.10%, max 22.10%, so the chosen fixed instance is not already solved by its start generator. Concorde's stored tour independently replays to the reported reference; its optimality is attributed to upstream data, not our own exact proof. All 512 Flip states are reachable, root bitmask 170 has minimum nine flips; its transition operators are commuting involutions and solving is equivalent to GF(2) linear algebra. This is deliberately a toy test of sparse RL feedback, not a claim about difficulty of classical Flip or general PUZZLES results.

A 64-group initialization screen (G16, H16, initial weight seed 42,
reset seed 20261128/rollout seed 20261129) had **zero improving rollouts in
both** full and subset modes. This makes a large untouched random-init
sweep scientifically weak. Registered follow-up: use exactly the published
current NICO gain-bias initialization (`delta_bias_init=10.0`) on the
existing normalized 2-opt gain feature in every arm, preserving all other
random components. Native gain information is already present equally in
both arms and in the original published method. This is a new baseline
initialization, registered before the follow-up; no reference tours or
oracle action labels are used. Measure whether this produces informative
groups; only recommend a full paired run if it does. Report the coefficient
and all 64-group counts; do not select an alternative gain bias from outcomes.

Gain-initialization follow-up: all 64 groups completed in each condition.
Full 60/64 informative, 740/1024 improving. Subset 41/64 informative,
465/1024 improving. Thus random city subsets *reduced* initial useful signal
for this gain-aware proxy, unlike the hoped-for gradient access result;
retained transfer could nonetheless occur. Do not interpret subsequent
positive results as proof of signal-rate mediation. The gain-aware proxy
does not implement NICO's tabu/history, teacher, architecture, PPO,
or model-generated warmup. Its result characterizes the proposed
subset mechanism under a deliberately simple learning model, not NICO
itself. The full NICO checkpoint was already audited in

Flip exact uniform-action success probability from root is 9!/9^9 =
0.0009366567. Under uniform solvable nonterminal resets the mean probability
is 0.01551231; under random goal-walk resets it is 0.07531050. This is a
concrete ~16.6× and ~80.4× increase in access to reward for an uninformed
policy. It does not establish learned full-task transfer. Full support,
distance histogram and frozen TSP group rewards saved in initial_audit.json.
Both deterministic repeated-seed smoke runs matched all weights and
checkpoint results, independent scalar replay passed on all evaluation

## Main CPU panel

Recommended: 8 paired seeds 42..49, 1000 updates, G16, H16 TSP/H9 Flip,
eight evaluation groups (128 trials) at each checkpoint, switch 300.
There are 24 TSP and 40 Flip independent jobs, combined 64 Python processes
on a shared Trillium full node. NumPy-only, one thread per worker.
TSP policy slots per arm: 256,000 and 300,800,000 padded candidate scores;
Flip slots: 144,000 and 1,296,000 padded candidate scores.
The 1000-update panel tests the mechanism on a nontrivial training budget;
avoid increasing merely to tighten confidence intervals. If all TSP arms
improve but subsets do not outperform ordinary, report a null for the
city-subset intervention. If near Flip learns but the switched policy
cannot solve from root, report a local-reward-access-only result.
Ordinary evaluation is always the full fixed task. For TSP, compare
paired terminal mean and best tour length and optimality gap versus
reference across arm and checkpoint. For Flip compare root hits.

## Common ordinary checkpoint fork (registered before implementation tests)

The user conjectures that ordinary RL beats expanded experience whenever
ordinary training already has a productive signal. Test a stronger null with
an identical ordinary-learning prefix in every arm, under the gain-aware TSP
initialization and, as a cheap complementary control, Flip. For the existing
three/five arms, use `--common-prefix`: train only from ordinary starts for
the first 30% of updates; then continue ordinary, enter the relevant expanded
start distribution permanently, or enter that distribution for the next 30%
and return to ordinary at 60%. All arms receive the same total updates and
padded policy/action slots, and common-prefix compute is charged in every
arm. Recompute the prefix independently for each branch, with exact equality
of serialized weights, Adam moments, reset RNG, rollout RNG and evaluation
records checked at the fork per domain/seed. This is mathematically identical
to cloning the checkpoint but intentionally duplicates computation. No
checkpoint is chosen by outcomes. Record whether average full-task quality
has improved since initialization and actual informative-group/positive-count
history at the fork; a case without observable progress does not test the
strong conjecture. Require an advantage over the continued-ordinary arm
on the same ordinary evaluation at the final budget for a claim of retained
transfer, and examine intermediate/post-restoration trajectories as well.
The old scratch-start experiment remains separately available. TSP subset
auxiliary objectives and actual feature/warmup arithmetic costs remain
explicit caveats; identical padded policy compute is not total wall-time.
